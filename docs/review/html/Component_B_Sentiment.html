Second review · academic prototype · not an operational system

Component B — Sentiment, emotion, and stance

How one post is read for feeling, emotion, stance, sarcasm, and the reaction bar.

This brief is for the teammate who presents the reading of each post. The models are used as published. We did not train them.

1. What this agent does

Component B reads the text of one post and writes one result row. It also scores the emoji reaction bar under the post. Those two readings stay separate. A crowd of likes does not rewrite the text label.

2. Where it sits

Figure 1. Posts land in Postgres. B reads one post at a time. C, D, and E recompute a time window. Ask searches the short summaries those agents write.

B is the only row runner. A post starts as pending. After B, it is processed. C, D, and E do not wait for that flag. They read a time window of posts.

Reaction barCount of 👍 🔥 and similar marks versus 👎 🤡 and similar marksA separate score. Cutoff 0.15. Unmapped emoji do not move it.

4. Why the models run one after another

The reference laptop has a 4 GB graphics card. Loading sentiment, emotion, and stance together exceeds that budget. The pipeline loads one model, finishes the batch, releases it, then loads the next. On a machine without a usable GPU the same order runs on the CPU. It is slower. The labels do not change because the device changed.

Figure 2. Memory is cleared between the three neural models. Sarcasm and the reaction bar need no graphics memory.

5. Stance rule

DistilBART asks whether the text supports or opposes the channel topic. If the best score is under 0.60, the stance is neutral or unclear. We do not force a side. The larger BART model is only a comparison for the report. It is not loaded in the demo, because it is about 1.6 GB.

6. What to show

Open Sentiment. Point at the positive, neutral, and negative bars, then at the reaction chips. Say the reaction row is the audience tapping the post, and the bars are the text model.

One sentence. “Sarcasm lowers our confidence. It never turns a positive label into a negative one.”
